# Project naming research: money, football, and lasting progress

**Date:** 26 October 2026  
**Status:** creative recommendations with preliminary public-web screening  
**Scope:** naming research only; no implementation or brand replacement
**Agent:** Codex

## Recommendation

Start the naming discussion with **Payformation**, **Paytactic**, **Goalstead**, and **Kobopitch**. Each offers a different advantage:

- **Payformation:** the closest expression of the original mechanism—pay organised into a formation.
- **Paytactic:** the most compact strategy-led alternative.
- **Goalstead:** the broadest platform for goals and sustained financial progress.
- **Kobopitch:** the clearest coined Nigerian money-and-football combination in this shortlist.

Keep **Payday Pitch** as the benchmark. It remains a strong, understandable, alliterative description of the concept. A replacement should improve something identifiable: memorability, spoken ease, differentiation, breadth, or the connection to the formation. Renaming is not automatically an improvement.

My first candidate to test is **Payformation**, with **Paytactic** alongside it. If the priority becomes a broader brand that can stretch beyond salary planning, test **Goalstead**. If an explicitly Nigerian identity matters most, test **Kobopitch** against the more literal **Naira Tactics**.

These recommendations are judgments about fit, not measured customer preferences. No proposed name is represented as legally cleared or commercially available.

## 1. Research basis and naming brief

This report uses the product idea and the existing research documents, particularly the [unified research report](2026-10-26-unified-research-report.md) and [fresh product, brand, and mobile research](fresh-product-brand-mobile-research.md). It does not inspect or derive suggestions from application code, components, routes, or the implemented identity.

The consolidated research records these relevant decisions: Nigeria first; football central to the recurring routine; a consumer subscription; manual planning initially; and a service that plans and records without holding or moving money. Those recorded decisions inform the naming brief. The visual recommendations elsewhere in the research do not determine the name.

The name should suggest some combination of:

1. **Money with a role:** salary and other income assigned to real purposes.
2. **Deliberate strategy:** a plan that can change when circumstances change.
3. **Football:** formation, lineup, pitch, tactics, and seasons.
4. **Continuity:** useful after the first payday and beyond the first goal.
5. **Approachability:** easy enough to say, remember, and recommend.

Avoid implying that the product is a lender, bank, investment fund, betting service, or real football club. Also avoid a name that requires exactly eleven financial categories: the consolidated research explicitly allows flexible purposes.

The central naming tension is useful: **a literal name explains the idea faster; a broader name leaves more room for the product to grow.** Both approaches appear below.

## 3. Developed name concepts

### Payformation

**Pronunciation:** pay-for-MAY-shun.  
**Intended meaning:** income given an organised formation. This is a proposed blend, not a dictionary definition.

The strongest feature of the name is that it points to what makes the product different. “Formation” can carry the football interface and the act of deliberately organising money. It also leaves room for more than a single salary source.

**Possible brand line:** “Your pay, in formation.”

**Plain-language descriptor:** “Salary planning through football formations.”

**How it sounds in use:** “I plan my salary with Payformation.”

**Possible extensions:** Payformation Plan; Payformation Season; Payformation Together. These are architecture examples, not additional features or packages being approved.

**Main test:** say it aloud without displaying the spelling. Can people repeat it, spell it, and distinguish it from “pay information”? A clever written blend is not enough if people cannot pass it on verbally.

**Screening result:** exact-name and finance/app queries did not surface an obvious dedicated product using the name. An unrelated dictionary-index occurrence appeared. Indexed Apple App Store and Google Play searches returned no results for the supplied name query. None of this establishes availability.

### Paytactic

**Pronunciation:** pay-TAK-tik.  
**Intended meaning:** a practical strategy for what to do with pay.

This is the tightest strategy-led candidate. It fits allocation, changes during a month, and reviewing what worked. Football can remain central without requiring a ball or pitch in every expression of the brand.

**Possible brand line:** “A game plan for every payday.”

**Plain-language descriptor:** “A football-inspired planner for your salary.”

**How it sounds in use:** “I use Paytactic to plan my month.”

**Possible extensions:** Paytactic Plan; Paytactic Goals; Paytactic Season.

**Main test:** does it sound like a payment-processing tool, a payroll service, or a personal planner? Also test whether people naturally add an “s.”

**Screening result:** exact-name, finance/app, and plural-variant searches did not surface an obvious dedicated product. Indexed store searches returned no results for the supplied name query. The result is preliminary.

### Goalstead

**Pronunciation:** GOHL-sted.  
**Intended meaning:** steady progress towards goals. This is the intended brand association, not an etymological claim.

This is the strongest option if the business should eventually encompass broader financial planning. It puts the user's progress ahead of the payday event. “Goal” can support football, while “stead” suggests stability and continuity.

**Possible brand line:** “A clearer plan for your next goal.”

**Plain-language descriptor:** “Money planning, shaped by football.”

**How it sounds in use:** “I use Goalstead to keep my money plans on track.”

**Possible extensions:** Goalstead Plan; Goalstead Season; Goalstead Together.

**Main test:** does it feel memorable and approachable, or like a property or financial-advisory firm? It needs a clear descriptor to establish the product category.

**Screening result:** searches found unrelated word-list and informal occurrences, rather than an obvious dedicated finance product. Indexed store searches returned no results for the supplied name query. The similar-looking **Goalward** is already used by other products, so the two should be included in later confusion testing. [Goalward first-party site](https://goalward.io/)

### Kobopitch

**Pronunciation:** KOH-boh-pitch.  
**Intended meaning:** a football pitch for organising money, with a Nigerian currency cue.

This is the most direct local blend in the developed shortlist. It is playful enough for a colourful identity while still connected to the actual concept. It does not rely on eleven positions, a single club, or a particular investment instrument.

**Possible brand line:** “Give every kobo a role.”

**Plain-language descriptor:** “A football formation for your money plan.”

**How it sounds in use:** “I set up my monthly plan on Kobopitch.”

**Possible extensions:** Kobopitch Plan; Kobopitch Goals; Kobopitch Season.

**Main test:** does “kobo” feel accessible and familiar or too small-scale for a premium subscription? Does the name travel well for diaspora users and people outside Nigeria?

**Screening result:** exact-name and finance/app queries did not surface an obvious dedicated product. Indexed store searches returned no results for the supplied name query. Broader spelling and phonetic checks remain open.

### Payday Tactics

**Pronunciation:** PAY-day TAK-tiks.  
**Intended meaning:** make a plan each time pay arrives, then keep adjusting it intelligently.

This is the strongest descriptive alternative if retaining “Payday” is desirable. “Tactics” communicates action and revision more directly than “Pitch,” while the football identity can remain in the product experience.

**Possible brand line:** “Get paid. Make a plan. Keep moving.”

**Plain-language descriptor:** “Salary planning with a football mindset.”

**How it sounds in use:** “I use Payday Tactics when my salary comes in.”

**Main test:** do people perceive it as an app, an educational newsletter, or an employer-payroll service? It will also need to communicate usefulness between paydays.

**Screening result:** an exact-phrase search did not surface an obvious dedicated product. This candidate did not receive the additional indexed store-search pass applied to the first four.

### Naira Tactics

**Pronunciation:** NY-rah TAK-tiks, subject to familiar local pronunciation.  
**Intended meaning:** deliberate choices about how naira serves the user's priorities.

This option prioritises immediate Nigerian relevance and readable words. It is less coined than Kobopitch, but also less tied to the visual formation itself.

**Possible brand line:** “Put a plan behind your pay.”

**Plain-language descriptor:** “A football-inspired salary planner.”

**How it sounds in use:** “Naira Tactics helps me plan what my salary needs to cover.”

**Main test:** does it sound like a trading-tips channel? The descriptor and demonstration must make the planning function clear.

**Screening result:** exact-name and app queries did not surface an obvious dedicated product. Currency specificity is a strategic trade-off, not a screened conflict.

## 6. Name architecture: what should name the company, app, and features?

The previous research contains good football language that does not all need to become the main brand. Keep a hierarchy:

| Layer                 | Naming job                                 | Example                                     |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------- |
| Main brand            | Memorable identity with room to develop    | Payformation                                |
| Descriptor            | State the category and mechanism clearly   | Salary planning through football formations |
| Monthly ritual        | Give payday planning a recognisable moment | Matchday                                    |
| Mid-cycle check       | Make a recurring action familiar           | Half-time review                            |
| Annual progress story | Give history a coherent narrative          | Season review                               |
| Subscription          | Explain the paid relationship              | Season Ticket, if it tests well             |

This is an illustrative arrangement. It does not rename the project or approve any of those features. A main brand such as Goalstead would need its descriptor to do more explanatory work than Payformation.

Avoid forcing the name into every button. The brand can be distinctive while an action remains “Save plan,” “Update amount,” or “Review month.”

## 7. How to choose the final name

Test a small set rather than asking people to rank all thirty-six. A useful first comparison is **Payformation, Paytactic, Goalstead, Kobopitch, and the existing Payday Pitch**.

### First pass: hear it

Say each name without showing its spelling. Ask the participant to repeat it and write what they heard. Record what they think the product does before explaining it.

This reveals problems that a wordmark can conceal: Payformation becoming “pay information,” Paytactic becoming “Paytactics,” or a name sounding like payroll, betting, or an investment app.

### Second pass: understand it

Show each name with the same factual descriptor and the same short product demonstration. Ask:

- What would you expect this app to help you do?
- Does it sound as though it holds or moves your money?
- Where does football appear in your understanding?
- Would you feel comfortable recommending it aloud?
- Does the name still fit goals, reviews, and household planning later?

### Third pass: remember it

Return to the names after another task and ask which ones participants remember unaided. Separate recall, category understanding, and personal preference. A funny name can be memorable while describing the wrong product.

Do not treat a small group as a statistically representative naming vote. Use the findings to identify confusion, pronunciation problems, and the strongest fit with the intended audience.

### Final checks for the chosen few

Once the founder narrows the set, investigate similar spellings and pronunciations, relevant company/trademark records, domains, handles, and app-store listings in the intended markets. WIPO's [Global Brand Database guidance](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/global-brand-database) describes its coverage and also advises checking national or regional registers. The database was consulted as a source about the next research step; no candidate searches were performed in it for this report.

Do not let a convenient domain settle the naming decision before the name's meaning and spoken use work.
