## How we use the interview

Work through up to four questions at a time. Use a real incident, amount or person wherever possible. “I do not know” becomes a testable assumption, not an invented answer. Each round updates the brief it affects before the next round.

The practical founder interview is complete for this handoff, including questions 24, 27, 28 and 29, answered on 27 October 2026. The record below captures the answers; prompts remain for traceability. Remaining identity and detailed behaviour questions are study inputs, not reasons to delay the sample prototype. The working name can label it. Next scope item: [feature 20](../scope/scope.md#20-matchday-prototype-and-pilot-preparation--planned--needs-a-decision).

## Round 1: The user and the job

1. **From research §6.1:** Who are the first ten people you can actually recruit? Give city, work, take home pay range, pay pattern, football interest and current tools. Which are close friends, and which could disagree freely?
2. **Added:** Walk through your last payday. What arrived, what had to be paid, what changed, and what did you do? Which single moment would make this app worth opening?
3. **From research §§1.4 and 3.6:** If someone understands the plan better as a list, should that be an equally useful experience, a supporting view, or outside the audience? Proposed starting point: equivalent pitch and list views with football identity.
4. **Report contradiction, §§0 and 1.5:** When essentials and required debt exceed confirmed income, do we block Attack, allow an acknowledged shortfall, or only warn? Proposed starting point: save an explicitly underfunded plan without claiming coverage.

## Round 2: Make the money model precise

5. **Adapted to the founder's answer:** When the first of your three salaries arrives, do you allocate it immediately or wait for all three? If one arrives a week late, should it update the same monthly plan or start a separate plan? Proposed starting point: one plan with separately recorded income arrivals and a clear expected/received distinction.
6. **Added from the debt scenario:** Is the majority going to debt a required payment, an extra amount chosen to clear it faster, or a mix? How do you decide what must remain for food, transport and data before adding extra debt repayment? Amounts and lender names are not needed to answer this product question.
7. **Follow up on unanswered part of Round 1:** In that payday, which would have helped most: an initial allocation across the three incomes, a limit on extra debt repayment that preserves essentials, a way to adjust later, or another intervention? Describe the decision you struggled with most; do not assume the app needs to maximise savings.
8. **From research §6.1, adapted:** Should Keeper appear from the first plan even if this month's contribution is zero, or be introduced later? If someone already holds emergency cash, should the app show that balance separately from new contributions? Proposed starting point: available from the first plan, with honest zero contributions and separate balances.

Later model probes still needed: how annual rent provisions and non optional family support gain protection; how corrections or edited commitments affect a past report and clean sheet. Their questions were moved out of the initial Round 2 queue to make room for the real payday scenario. They remain open in the product brief.

## Round 3: What remains, and what the formation means

9. **Adapted to Round 2:** Before all three salaries arrive, should the remainder describe only received pay, the expected full month, or both separately? Proposed starting point: show received pay remainder and a distinct forecast, neither presented as a bank balance.
10. **Adapted to Round 2:** When an essential estimate rises, should the app first suggest reducing an uncompleted additional debt repayment or other optional allocation, protect that extra repayment, or ask without a suggested priority? The user retains the final choice. A repayment already recorded cannot simply be reallocated.
11. **Adapted to Round 2:** What food tracking would the founder actually maintain: a revisable monthly estimate, a weekly allowance, or individual purchases? How is food managed today? Do not infer that an unpredictable essential is optional.
12. **From research §1.4:** Choose between four lines with flexible real purposes, five money zones with a separate spending Bench, or a literal eleven player financial model. The proposed four lines are Keeper for reserve, Defence for essentials and required debt, Midfield for flexible spending and nearer goals, and Attack for longer goals and wealth contributions. Family support may be protected as essential when appropriate. The four line model is not approved merely because Keeper is approved.

## Round 4: Cut a first release and a business promise

13. **From research §§1.7 and 3.8:** What must work for you to invite the first user: planning, checklist, adjustments, review, reminders, sharing or something else? If only the complete basic loop fits, which extras can wait?
14. **From research §6.1:** Which result would convince you after two pay cycles: voluntary return, completed commitments, less uncertainty, or another observable outcome? Proposed primary signal: voluntary useful return, with self reported outcomes kept separate.
15. **Report contradiction, §§1.7 and 1.9:** What is meaningfully premium if basic planning and original records stay free? Should recording side income be free while separate squad views are paid? Proposed starting point: do not charge for accurately entering money the user actually receives.
16. **Added:** What are your weekly time budget, cash ceiling, target pilot date and recruitment capacity? Are there existing users or commitments we need to account for? Which date is fixed, if any?

Follow up **15a:** Should future bank connected saved rules prepare a draft for review or automatically save a plan, with all money staying in the bank? Could manual income entry plus saved rules be enough for the initial experiment? Proposed starting point: review before saving, and test rules manually before a live bank integration.

Follow up **16a:** Does “this week” mean a clickable prototype and pilot recruitment, a usable private pilot with requested features, or a public release? Proposed immediate milestone: prototype and recruitment. The full requested release remains the agreed desired scope unless the founder revises it.

## Round 5: Name and creative boundaries

17. **From naming §7:** Which names do you love or veto, and why? Is a money word mandatory, or can the descriptor carry that meaning? Does the company need the same name as the app?
18. **From naming §7:** Is this a distinctly Nigerian brand with possible expansion, or a broadly international brand from day one? What would make you comfortable keeping Payday Pitch despite the reported overlap?
19. **From research §6.1:** What should “premium” feel like: a calm financial document, a modern football club, or a colourful sports tool? Name two references you want to keep and one you reject, explaining the effect rather than just the colour.
20. **From research §7:** How much can we spend on type and illustration, and when? Proposed starting point: verify an available numeral pairing before commissioning a fork; use only prototype essential assets initially.

## Later topics: Trust, retention and social concepts

21. **Added:** Before account creation, should people use sample figures or their real pay? On a shared phone, what should remain after closing the tab? Proposed starting point for initial testing: samples until draft storage and deletion behaviour are decided.
22. **From research §§1.8 and 6.1:** Why would someone join a mini league if progress is self reported? What does a host pay for? Proposed starting point: defer leagues; if tested later, compare adherence rather than salary or allocation aggressiveness.
23. **Added:** A user has a hard month and no clean sheet. What useful reason do they have to return? Which reminder would feel helpful, and which would feel like pressure? Ask for the exact words they would tolerate.
24. **From research §§4.2 and 4.8:** Where would the first user actually hear about this, and would they share a formation? Are percentages safe to share when relatives might infer salary or obligations? Proposed starting point: sharing optional, with an explicit preview and sensitive details hidden.

## Prototype decisions added from the interview

25. **Clean sheet definition:** Should all essentials and required debt payments due in the period be recorded as paid, every allocation including savings be completed, or just the plan be saved? Proposed starting point: all due essentials and required debt payments recorded as paid. Optional contributions do not disqualify; missing information stays unknown; an empty plan earns no award.
26. **Planning period:** When pay arrives at the end of October to cover November bills, is this the November plan, October plan, or a payday date range? Proposed starting point: name the month whose commitments the money covers. This does not assume the founder's answer or decide late income behaviour in advance.

## Remaining practical pilot round

**24. Sharing follow up:** choose cards that always omit amounts, cards that omit amounts by default but allow a deliberate amount bearing version after preview, or generic achievement badges only. Both financial card options start with shape/line percentages and hide personal purpose names and identity. The chat clarified that the first option keeps amounts off every card; the second permits them only after an explicit preview. Exact privacy defaults are not agreed until answered.

**27. Reminders:** Which of expected payday, upcoming essentials, weekly food check and month review would the founder welcome? Which would annoy them, and how often is acceptable? Notification channels and implementation come later.

**28. Recruitment:** how many friends can the founder ask this week? Describe three anonymous likely testers by work/pay pattern, football interest and current planning tool, with broad income ranges only if known. No names or contact details are requested. This tests whether the candidate persona and pilot plan are reachable.

**29. Payment timing:** record a free two pay cycle pilot followed by a working premium benefit test, allow an earlier optional paid test if a premium benefit works, or keep timing open. The current recommendation is free pilot first; no response means it remains a recommendation.

## Prototype contract

The reports propose five anchor screens, but the agreed flow needs explicit entry states. Start with a labelled sample preview and a simulated account step. Then use five working anchors: personal setup, builder, review/saved, Today, and period report. Include checklist, adjustment comparison, shortfall and weekly food check interactions. Show representative goal, reminder, share preview and clean sheet states for the requested extras. These can be short branches rather than new navigation destinations.

Use November as the sample plan label when late October pay covers November commitments. Keep actual sample receipt dates visible. Show both received and forecast remainders. A sample clean sheet counts essentials and required debt recorded as paid; show an incomplete data state separately, and do not disqualify a user for no optional contribution. Use Matchday's colourful modules and Nigerian context, with a focused Floodlight comparison if capacity permits. No name winner or final font purchase is required for this study.

Use sample data first. Ask each participant to:

1. Confirm received rather than expected pay and identify what must be paid next.
2. Build and save a plan; explain whether money moved and what they must do elsewhere.
3. Record one action, then correct it.
4. Handle a salary shortfall and a new expense, explaining which purpose loses funding.
5. Review what is known versus missing and prepare the next cycle without counting money twice.

Record task completion, time, prompting, mistakes, false transfer beliefs and the participant's own explanation. Find the next due action within five seconds and complete a returning plan in roughly 90 seconds are report hypotheses, not launch promises. Set decision thresholds before the session. A custody misconception or hidden shortfall blocks this flow's pilot until corrected and rechecked.

Include a person with limited football knowledge, one with late or split pay, and participants using modest phones or larger text where available. Check mobile first and a wider viewport early. Do not infer accessibility from a visual preference test.

## Pilot and pricing evidence

Record each participant's actual next expected payday so late salary does not count as churn. Track activation, opportunity to return, useful return, missing data and reasons for non return. Ask what changed in their real decisions; checklist ticks alone do not establish financial resilience. Get explicit permission for reminders, personal data collection and any quotes used later.

The founder agrees that early success includes voluntary return and understanding what remains and what to do next. Wealth building alongside debt clearance is the longer term goal. Where data is available, follow reserve or goal contributions and debt balances separately. Do not turn any debt payment into assumed principal reduction, or two cycles of self reports into proof of lasting wealth.

The research's ₦1,200 to ₦2,000 monthly range is only a proposed test band. Do not ask “Would you pay?” alone. First identify a capability they used and would miss, then test a concrete offer with clear price and renewal terms. Do not collect payment or promise unbuilt functionality as part of this documentation task.

**Payment sequence agreed in question 29:** first run a free two pay cycle pilot, then a paid benefit test. Discuss pricing during the pilot; explicit paid opt in needs a chosen benefit that works. A free pilot tests behaviour, not willingness to pay. No automatic conversion or charge for bank automation while only a manual simulation exists. The benefit, price and date still need a concrete offer.

**Low cost experiment recommendation:** after manual income entry, apply saved preferences to a clearly labelled draft and let the participant review it. This tests the allocation rule's usefulness before a live bank feed. The future bank linked interaction is agreed as detected income preparing a draft for review; consent, disconnect, data freshness and correction behaviour remain to specify. Consult the product brief's primary sources; no provider or live integration is approved here.

**This week's milestone, agreed in 16a:** clickable flow prototype and recruit the three candidate friends, at minimal cost. Recommended prototype coverage: the core flow and representative extras, led by Matchday's colourful modules, with a focused Floodlight comparison if capacity permits. The full loop plus reminders, share cards, goals and celebrations remain the desired release scope. Exact spend ceiling and working hours are unknown. A clickable flow with sample data is distinct from the later usable product needed for a real two pay cycle pilot.

The prototype's share preview must show only shape and line percentages, hiding amounts, purpose names and identity. Include settings or examples for all four reminder moments; mark any sample timing as a test assumption. Do not interpret the founder welcoming reminders as a tested notification frequency.

Before recruitment, assign who schedules sessions, facilitates, records findings and maintains decisions. Keep recruiting and outreach in the founder's hands unless explicitly delegated.
